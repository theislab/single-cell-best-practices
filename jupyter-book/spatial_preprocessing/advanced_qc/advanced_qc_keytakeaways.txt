1
Quality control of image-based spatial data spans three levels: single-molecule observations, cell segmentation and the image itself.
Transcripts outside cells, low-quality transcripts and negative control probes should be inspected spatially, because artifacts often cluster in specific regions or cell types.

2
Segmentation collapses the tissue volume into 2D, so cells at different depths can merge into so called vertical doublets, which can be detected by comparing transcript patterns between the top and bottom z-levels.

3
Ambient RNA in spatial assays can be spatially localized, and genes with a Moran's I close to 0 may indicate contamination.

4
Positive and negative marker genes should be checked for the expected spatial distribution to confirm biological credibility.

5
Conventional single-cell QC metrics such as total counts, gene counts and cell and nucleus area also reveal segmentation errors.

6
Invalid cell and nucleus geometries should be corrected or removed.

7
Otsu thresholding of pixel intensities helps flag low-signal, blurry or out-of-focus regions.

8
H&E artifact detection tools struggle on post-Xenium H&E stains, so their masks should only guide the inspection of problematic regions.
