Integrating AIR and transcriptomics

🧠 Key takeaways

Paired receptor and gene expression data link the clonal history and specificity of cells to their state.

Clonotype modularity identifies clonotypes whose cells share a transcriptional state.

Joint embeddings of receptors and gene expression have not been independently benchmarked and should be used to generate hypotheses.

Motivation¶

Paired receptor and gene expression measurements link the clonal history and specificity of a cell to its state. Cells of one clonotype share their antigen and often their fate, which is partly determined by the receptor itself Lagattuta et al., 2022. In the previous chapters, we used the transcriptomic cell types to interpret clonotypes, and here we use the clonotypes to interpret the gene expression.

Clonotypes in the gene expression space¶

We normalize the gene expression and build a neighborhood graph on the principal components that the authors corrected for batch effects between the sequencing runs.

gex = mdata_tcr["gex"]
sc.pp.normalize_total(gex)
sc.pp.log1p(gex)
sc.pp.neighbors(gex, use_rep="X_pca_harmony")
sc.tl.umap(gex)

To color the embedding by clonal expansion, we copy it from the airr modality to the gene expression.

gex.obs["clonal_expansion"] = mdata_tcr.obs["airr:clonal_expansion"]
sc.pl.umap(gex, color=["cell_type_coarse", "clonal_expansion"], wspace=0.3)

Expanded clonotypes are concentrated in one region of the CD8+ T cells, whereas CD4+ T cells are rarely expanded. Cells that lack one of the two chains have no clonotype (nan).

Clonotype modularity¶

Clonotype modularity tests whether the cells of a clonotype are more connected in the neighborhood graph of the gene expression than expected by chance, following the idea of CoNGA Schattgen et al., 2022. Clonotypes with a high modularity share a transcriptional state, whereas clonotypes whose cells are spread across states have differentiated into several fates.

ir.tl.clonotype_modularity(mdata_tcr, target_col="airr:clone_id")
mdata_tcr.pull_obs()

Most clonotypes with a significant modularity consist of terminal effector or effector memory CD8+ T cells, whose expanded clones share an effector state.

Gene expression of expanded T cells¶

Groups defined by the receptors can be compared like any other groups of cells. We compare expanded and non-expanded CD8+ T cells.

cd8 = gex[gex.obs["cell_type_coarse"] == "CD8"].copy()
cd8.obs["expanded"] = (cd8.obs["clonal_expansion"] == "> 2").astype(str)
sc.tl.rank_genes_groups(cd8, "expanded", groups=["True"], reference="False")
sc.pl.rank_genes_groups(cd8, n_genes=15)

Expanded CD8+ T cells express cytotoxic genes such as NKG7, GZMH, GNLY and PRF1, consistent with their effector state. This comparison treats cells as independent samples, which suffices to characterize groups of cells. Comparisons between conditions, such as severity groups, should instead aggregate the cells of each donor into pseudobulk samples, as described in the differential gene expression chapter Squair et al., 2021.

Joint embeddings¶

Several methods learn a joint representation of receptor sequences and gene expression. CoNGA relates the neighborhood graphs of TCR and gene expression similarity Schattgen et al., 2022, TESSA and its BCR counterpart Benisse let receptor similarity constrain the expression of clonotypes Zhang et al., 2021Zhang et al., 2022, and mvTCR embeds TCRs and gene expression with a multimodal variational autoencoder Drost et al., 2024. Their authors showed that joint representations capture specificity better than either modality alone, but no independent benchmark of these methods exists yet. We therefore recommend starting with the transparent analyses shown above and using joint embeddings to generate hypotheses, for example about groups of clonotypes that may share an antigen.
